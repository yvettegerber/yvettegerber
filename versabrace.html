The problem of Osteoarthritis spans multiple disciplines. Its impact is significant both nationally and globally, with projections indicating that its prevalence will continue to rise. Medically, Osteoarthritis develops in varied ways and produces a pain response that is challenging to interpret and manage. Because the lived experience of Osteoarthritis often depends on the availability and quality of treatment, access to medical care plays a crucial role in shaping outcomes. Individuals with lower socioeconomic status face greater financial obstacles, limited access to care, and provider bias, all of which contribute to a higher overall burden of the condition.

Clinically, braces mitigates the heterogenous development and inability to precisely monitor and model pain response. However, current brace designs fall short in addressing the full scope of the problem. While braces may be less expensive than other treatments, they are still not financially accessible for everyone, particularly those with limited income. Since Osteoarthritis often affects multiple joints in the body, purchasing a separate brace for each affected area can create additional financial strain. Many specialized braces also require fitting by a physician, which introduces further barriers for individuals who may face challenges related to geography, cost, or provider discrimination. There is a clear need for modular brace solutions, as modularity, affordability, and accessibility go hand in hand.

A brace designed to adapt to multiple body locations and offer various levels of support would significantly reduce costs by combining the functions of several braces into a single product. One potential solution is VersaBrace, a wrap-style design, where users apply a base brace and layer additional wrapping according to specific patterns tailored to different joints and levels of support.

The design for the wrap pattern is inspired by kinesiological research, which allows cheaper fabrics to be used in a way that provides comparable support to more advanced materials at a fraction of the cost. Such a design allows individuals to customize their treatment independently and more affordably, while also reducing reliance on medical professionals for fitting. Further, this type of design is more inclusive, accommodating a wide range of pain levels and condition development, both within one person over time and across different individuals. By addressing both financial limitations and the medical heterogeneity of Osteoarthritis, the VersaBrace solution could effectively serve a broader population and meet the varied needs associated with the condition.
