Engineering and Entrepreneurship: VersaBrace

❮ ❯

Osteoarthritis is a multidisciplinary problem with significant, growing impact worldwide. It develops in varied ways and produces a pain response that's hard to interpret and manage. Since outcomes depend heavily on access to care, those with lower socioeconomic status face greater financial barriers, limited access, and provider bias, which raises their overall disease burden.

Braces help address this variability, but current designs fall short. Even when cheaper than other treatments, they're often financially inaccessible since Osteoarthritis frequently affects multiple joints, requiring separate braces for each. Many also require physician fitting, adding barriers tied to geography, cost, or bias. Modularity, affordability, and accessibility are thus closely linked needs.

A brace adaptable to multiple body locations and support levels would cut costs by combining several braces into one and allowing for different styles to fit varied lifestyle needs. VersaBrace, a wrap-style design, does this: users apply a base brace and layer wrapping in patterns tailored to different joints and support needs, drawing on kinesiological research to let cheaper fabrics perform comparably to costlier materials.

This lets patients customize treatment independently and affordably, without professional fitting, while accommodating varied pain levels and disease progression over time and across individuals. By addressing financial and medical heterogeneity together, VersaBrace could serve a broader population with more varied needs.
